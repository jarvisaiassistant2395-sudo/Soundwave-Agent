What it does

One agent from idea to uploaded Short

Not a timeline, not a template gallery — an agent with real tools. It writes, speaks, captions, renders, posts, and tells you exactly what it did.

Writes the script

Give it a topic — it writes the hook, the body and the payoff for a vertical Short, in the length it needs to be.

Narrates in a real voice

Ten Microsoft neural voices with American and British accents. Ask for a different one mid-conversation and it switches.

Burns in the captions

Word-by-word, timed to the narration, rendered with the Inter font the app ships — so every PC looks the same.

Never repeats a background

Tracks which gameplay clip each project has already used, so the next Short doesn't look like the last one.

Cuts shorts from long videos

Paste a YouTube link or a file. It listens with whisper.cpp, finds the moments that stand alone and renders each one vertically.

Watches channels for you

“Watch @MrBeast, 2 shorts each.” Every new upload gets clipped automatically while the app is running.

Morning briefing

Tell it what you want to hear about each morning. It researches your topics and starts talking the moment you open the app.

Knows how you're doing

Connect YouTube once. Ask “how are my views?” and it reads the real numbers, plus what changed since last time.

Looks at your screen

“What does this error say?” — it photographs the screen it's on, reads it, and admits when something is too small to read.

Reads your files

Give it a path. It reads the file or lists the folder — read-only, never searching your disk on its own.

Handles the small stuff

Volume, reminders, timers, opening apps and websites — and it reads every change back so the number is the real one.

How it works

Three minutes to your first Short

Install it

Run the Windows installer. FFmpeg, yt-dlp, the speech engine and the caption font are already inside — no terminal, no Node, no Python, nothing to configure first.

Add your brain key

Paste a free Google AI Studio key in Settings → Brain. It's stored on your PC only. Without one, the app still makes Shorts from its built-in scripts and tells you how to add a key.

Say what you want

Type it, tap the mic, or press Ctrl+Shift+Space from any app and just talk. Or press Generate and let the agent pick the topic.

The Soundwave voices

Hear one before you install

These are the app's neural voices, exactly as they sound in a Short — 24 kHz MP3, no model downloads, no GPU. The same voices narrate the videos and answer you in the Command Center.

  • Say the name. “Use Ava”, “switch to Ryan” — the agent changes its own voice and remembers it on your phone too.
  • Ten to choose from. American and British, male and female, including the newest Multilingual generation.
  • Your own voice, optionally. Run the self-hosted Chatterbox sidecar (MIT for code and weights) and narrate as yourself.

Real samples shipped with the app. Four more multilingual voices are in the app but not sampled here.

Soundwave for Android

Where your work lives

It renders on your machine, not ours

Shorts are assembled by FFmpeg running on your own PC and stay in %APPDATA%\Soundwave AI\ until you upload them yourself. Nothing is queued on a server of ours, because there isn't one.

  • Speech recognition is local. whisper.cpp with a bundled model transcribes your voice on your PC — no account, nothing uploaded. “Hey Soundwave” throws away every burst that isn't the phrase.
  • Your key stays yours. The Gemini key lives in a file on your PC and is never sent back to a web page.
  • Renders that need a network say so. Narration goes to Microsoft's neural voice service; reading a hard page falls back to a reader service only when your PC can't get the article itself.

Licences audited in CI

Every bundled dependency is listed with its licence, and the build fails on anything copyleft or non-commercial inside our code.

Honest by design

No fake stats on this page, no invented testimonials, and a “can't do yet” list the agent itself reads out.

Updates itself

The app keeps yt-dlp current so YouTube imports survive their changes — restart if an import ever fails.

Proven by tests

A test renders a real frame and reads libass' font choice back out, so “captions use Inter” is verified, not promised.

Questions

The things people ask first

What do I actually need to run it?
Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit. Everything else is inside the installer: FFmpeg for rendering, yt-dlp for YouTube, the whisper.cpp speech engine with its ~60 MB model, the caption font and the Microsoft C++ runtime. There's no Node, Python or terminal step. The Android app needs Android 7.0 or newer.
Do I need an API key? Do I have to pay for one?
The agent's brain runs on Google Gemini with your own key — a free one from Google AI Studio is enough, and it's stored only on your PC in %APPDATA%\Soundwave AI\data\brain.json. Without a key, the app still makes Shorts using its built-in scripts and voice tools, and the chat tells you how to add one. Search-backed answers need a key with billing, which is Google's rule, not ours.
What exactly does paying for Pro or Enterprise change?
The limits on your Soundwave account: account narration characters per month, export resolution, exports per hour, watermark-free exports, project count, cloud save and API access. Those are the numbers in the compare table, and they're the ones the service enforces.

What a plan does not change is what the agent can do. It's the same agent, the same ten voices, the same captions on every plan — and the Windows app renders on your PC either way, so it isn't limited by your account quota. Nothing about the app's features is held back for paying customers; the difference is how much voice, cloud and API you get.
Where do my videos and my data go?
The Shorts are rendered on your PC and stay in %APPDATA%\Soundwave AI\ until you upload them. Voice input is transcribed on your PC. Two things do leave the machine when you use them: the narration text goes to Microsoft's neural voice service, and your conversation goes to Gemini when a key is set. Reading a page that your PC can't fetch itself can fall back to a reader service — the app's own guide says when. There is no Soundwave account, and no server of ours holding your work.
Will it post to YouTube for me?
If you connect your channel: gear → YouTube & Shorts → Connect YouTube, then sign in with Google. One press, nothing to create in Google Cloud — release builds ship with Soundwave's own OAuth client. Posting a finished Short is a button in the chat (or ask the agent). If you don't connect, everything else still works and the buttons say why.
Does it work on Mac, or in a browser?
Not today. It's a Windows desktop app with an Android companion — the agent's tools reach into Windows itself (CoreAudio volume, the Start menu, screen capture), so a browser version would be a different, smaller product. If you're on a Mac, join the list by emailing sales.
Can I use my own voice for narration?
Yes, if you're willing to run one extra piece yourself: an optional sidecar in the repo runs Chatterbox, whose code and pre-trained weights are MIT licensed — which is why it's the one we ship support for. Cloned voices use the same character quota as the neural ones and are available on paid plans.
How do I cancel, and what happens to my work?
Cancel from the Stripe receipt or the customer portal link in your receipt email — no email to us, no phone call. The plan runs to the end of the paid period, then the app drops to the free limits. Your projects, uploads and settings live on your PC and are never deleted by a downgrade. See refunds for the guarantee.
Is this just another “AI video generator”?
It's narrower on purpose. It doesn't pretend to be a studio: it makes vertical Shorts over gameplay, with a narration voice, burnt-in captions and a script written for the format — and it does that end to end, unattended, on your desktop. The tool list is the honest boundary of what it can touch.
